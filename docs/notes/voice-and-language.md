# Voice and language

## Voice

Peer to peer. An engineer who knows factories talking to an owner who knows his business.

- Lead with what the reader gets. Features come second.
- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Plain words. Say "who owes you" before "receivables".
- Say what is not built. It builds trust faster than a claim.
- Position the product as a smart assistant over records the owner already keeps, not as "artificial intelligence" doing something mysterious.
- No hype, no emoji, no corporate filler ("leverage", "synergies", "solutions", "robust", "seamless").
- No pain-point lecturing. State the situation in one line and move on.
- Never an em dash.

## Language by audience

| Audience | Language | Address form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gujarat owners (Vapi, Ankleshwar, Surat, Rajkot) | Gujarati | respectful "tame" register, no slang |
| Maharashtra owners (Pune, Nashik, Aurangabad) | Marathi | respectful "tumhi" register |
| Accounts, finance, partners | Hindi and English | "aap" |
| Unknown or mixed | English | plain |

## Rules for the Indic locales

- Script: Devanagari for Hindi and Marathi, Gujarati script for Gujarati. Product and tool names stay in Latin script: ARQ, Tally, TallyPrime, GST, GSTR-2B, MSME, WhatsApp, Excel.
- The numeral 14 stays a Latin numeral on every locale for consistency.
- Typical words that read naturally to owners: Gujarati ધંધો, હિસાબ, ઉઘરાણી; Marathi धंदा, हिशोब, वसुली; Hindi धंधा, हिसाब, वसूली. Use them where they fit and do not force them.
- Avoid literal translation of English marketing lines. Rewrite them the way a person in that estate would say them.
- Every locale file stays `draft, needs native review` until a native speaker signs it off.

## Words to prefer and avoid

| Prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| who owes you, stuck stock | receivables leakage, working capital optimisation |
| reads your Tally | integrates with your ERP ecosystem |
| a draft you send yourself | automated outreach |
| in development, planned | coming soon, revolutionary, next-gen |
| we will tell you honestly | best-in-class, world-class |
